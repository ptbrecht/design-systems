# Local Energy — voice notes

Applies [`../writing/voice.md`](../writing/voice.md) to localenergy.com. That file wins on headlines and on AI. This file only says how the rule lands on this site.

The name, the headline, and the tagline below are Peter’s. The rest is the working note for posts and UI copy.

## Locked lines

- **Name:** Local Energy. Not “Local Energy podcast.” The show is something they do. The brand is the shop.
- **Headline (home hero):** Oil & gas shop talk with Peter Brecht and Wade Spear.
- **Retired:** “Supporting Independent Energy.” is no longer the hero line (approved 2026-10-19 with the human-feel plan).

Use the name alone in the wordmark, the browser title’s brand, the nav, and the social card. “Podcast” is fine in a sentence when you mean the audio show (“Listen to the podcast”). It is not fine in the logo lockup. The lockup carries the red star as a superscript. A sentence does not. Do not type a star into body copy.

## Headlines

Harry Dry, from `writing/voice.md`: **2–6 words, concrete.** Name a thing a person can picture.

The home hero line is the shop-talk line above; it is the one exception to the 2–6 word test because it names the people. Article headlines follow the test. They are not taglines, and they are not sentences with a colon.

Do not cut the hero line down, and do not reuse it as an article h1.

Display type is one line on the home page. Article titles use h1, still 2–6 words, still concrete. A clever abstract title is a failed headline.

## Blog and shop-talk copy

Write the way `writing/voice.md` teaches Peter to write: direct, then the lesson, in plain words. Shop talk, not a brand film and not a trade-journal lede.

- Open on a moment or a claim a reader can see. Not on a thesis about “the future of energy.”
- Teach in one or two plain sentences, then a concrete picture.
- Peter and Wade are the people on the show. Name them. Don’t turn the site into a faceless company.
- The writing is Local Energy. The insurance brokerage voice in `writing/chuck-style.md` and the Falcon West Energy site voice in `writing/energy.md` are other brands. Do not import their promises, their product names, or their sign-off.

UI copy is short and literal: “Listen on Spotify”, “Read the note”, “Your name”. Sentence case. No marketing verbs stacked in a button.

## Patterns that read as machine-written

Delete these on sight, in posts, pages, and UI copy:

- “isn’t just X, it’s Y” / “not just X: it’s Y”.
- “Whether you’re X or Y…”.
- Anaphora runs (“We talk to… We sit down with… We share…”).
- Three-item lists used for rhythm rather than because there are three things; four-item audience lists (“landmen, engineers, producers, and operators”).
- Buzzwords: navigate, frontier, unlock, discover, explore, elevate, empower, journey, landscape, game-changer, seamless, cutting-edge.
- Two competing headlines side by side; a headline restated by the paragraph under it.
- Over-explaining: a sentence that tells the reader what the next sentence will say.

**Em dashes:** at most **one per page**. Use a period or a comma.

**Eyebrows and labels:** at most one label per page, sentence case, never red. No uppercase kickers.

## Personal touches (approved)

Specific, true, and small. Pick up to two per page, placed low (intro sign-off or footer):

- “Recorded in California and Oklahoma.”
- A running episode count or subscriber count, only if it is current.
- A guest’s hometown on the episode card.
- A short sign-off under the intro: “– Peter & Wade”.

Write in first person where Peter or Wade is speaking. Use real numbers and places over adjectives.

## AI

Never personify AI. Call it a tool or a program. Not a teammate, not “he” or “she,” not a character in the shop. This is house law from `writing/voice.md` and it applies to posts, captions, buttons, and empty states.
